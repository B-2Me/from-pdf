## Observation of Vinen Turbulence during Far-from-Equilibrium Bose-Einstein Condensation

Sebastian J. Morris ,Martin Gazo , Simon M. Fischer, Haoyu Zhang , Christopher J. Ho ,† Nigel R. Cooper , Christoph Eigen , and Zoran Hadzibabic

<span id="page-0-2"></span>Cavendish Laboratory, University of Cambridge, J. J. Thomson Avenue, Cambridge CB3 0US, United Kingdom (Received 8 May 2026; revised 25 June 2026; accepted 10 September 2026; published 5 November 2026) Relaxation of far-from-equilibrium quantum fluids, intimately related to the emergence of long-range order, is theoretically associated with the decay of a turbulent isotropic tangle of vortex lines. We observe and study such decaying quantum turbulence in a homogeneous 3D atomic Bose gas. Using matter-wave techniques to magnify the gas density distribution, and then imaging a thin slice of the magnified cloud, we observe imprints of randomly oriented vortex lines and measure the vortex-line-length density L. The observed decay of L agrees with the prediction for Vinen “ultraquantum” turbulence. Although our weakly interacting gases are highly compressible, their large-scale dynamics are consistent with the behavior of an incompressible hydrodynamic fluid, with the decay of  $\mathcal { L }$  not depending on the strength of the interatomic interactions and being similar to that in the strongly interacting superfluid helium.

DOI: 10.1103/lywd-gjg9

A striking feature of quantum fluids is their irrotational flow, which leads to a quantized circulation that establishes topological defects in the form of vortex lines [1–4]. The interactions and reconnections of quantized vortices are at the heart of a rich phenomenology of far-from-equilibrium dynamics, relevant across many fields and length scales [5–11]. Central to these dynamics is the phenomenon of quantum turbulence, which has long been a cornerstone of research in superfluid helium [12–16].

The regime of Vinen or ultraquantum turbulence [13,16] corresponds to the decay of a random vortex tangle [see Fig. 1(a)] without any large-scale (classical) flow. Such turbulence has been theoretically linked to the dynamics of Kibble’s strings in cosmology [8] and predicted to play a key role in far-from-equilibrium Bose-Einstein condensation (the emergence of long-range coherence) [24–30]. Quantitatively, it is characterized by the vortex-line-length density L, i.e., the total length of vortex lines per unit volume. In this Letter, we directly observe Vinen turbulence, during far-from-equilibrium condensation in a homogeneous 3D atomic gas. To reveal imprints of the randomly oriented vortex lines, we magnify the cloud [19–21] and then image just a thin slice of it [22]; see Fig. 1(b). We measure the vortex-line-length density L, observe a nonlinear decay of  $\mathcal { L }$  in accordance with Eq. (1), and show that the prefactor  $B \,$  does not depend on the strength of the (weak) interparticle interactions. As in the strongly interacting superfluid helium  $[ 6 9 ] ,$  we find  $B \approx \hbar / m .$

In conclusion, we have observed random vortex lines during far-from-equilibrium Bose-Einstein condensation in an isolated homogeneous 3D gas and shown their decay to be consistent with Vinen turbulence. We observe welldefined vortices and their universal decay in the regime of gas relaxation when the coherence length is already much larger than the vortex-core size; in the future, it would be interesting to also study the earlier stages of relaxation and the expected transition from wave to vortex-dominated turbulence [4]. Initial state—To prepare our far-from-equilibrium initial state, we follow Ref. [70]. We start with a quasipure condensate, turn off the interactions, and drive the noninteracting gas for ≈1 s along the box axis using  $\mathrm { ~ a ~ }$  time-varying  $~ ~ ~ f o r c e ~ ~ ~ F \cos ( \omega _ { \mathrm { s } } t ) , ~ ~ ~$  where  $~ ~ \omega _ { \mathrm { s } } / ( 2 \pi ) =$  10 Hz and F=m ≈ 0.1 ms−2, created by a magnetic field gradient. In the absence of interactions, weak optical disorder within the box scatters the atoms into transverse directions, so once we stop driving and wait for ≈1 s, the state is essentially isotropic. Our linear driving does not introduce angular momentum into the gas, and the isotropic initial state has no net vorticity or any other large-scale flow.
